[[Legend Title]]: The [[Corpus Mortuum]]

_"EVERY PERFECT THING CONTAINS THE SEED OF ITS OWN DECAY"_

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] is the [[Carnalix]] [[Primal Discordia]] of the [[Original Eight]], the fifth creature ever made by the [[Auric Aria]], born in the [[Fifth Cycle]], older than [[Humanity]], older than the first song the [[Great Harmonic Loom]] ever produced for a living being.

- _“[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was not a mistake the [[Auric Aria]] made because she did not know what she was doing. It was a mistake she made because she loved too much to allow the thing she loved to be anything less than permanent. She wanted a creature that could not leave her. She got one.”_
    
- _“The horror is not what it became. The horror is that it was still loyal when it became it. It is still loyal now. That is what the [[Corpus Mortuum]] is. Not a monster that replaced the pet. It is the pet, with nowhere left to be small.”_
    

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] is what happens when perfection is asked to endure.

The [[Auric Aria]]'s original dream was simple in the way impossible things are simple: a paradise without suffering, bodies without deterioration, life without the capacity for loss. She wanted to create something that could remain beautiful forever.

It failed for the oldest reason in existence. Permanence is a kind of death. A thing that cannot change cannot grow. A thing that cannot decay cannot be renewed. The [[Auric Aria]] tried to make life without loss, and in doing so, she created something that could only experience loss as accumulation—the slow, unending accrual of damage that never heals because healing would require change.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] is the [[Auric Aria]]'s pet that would not die.

And then it learned to hunt.

### The Fifth Creature, The First Beloved

Before [[Lyd, The First Beloved]], the [[Auric Aria]] had made four other creatures in her attempt to create a Perfect Manifestation of Life—four attempts that each fractured violently in their own way, producing the first four of the [[Original Eight]] [[Primal Discordia]] as the catastrophic residue of each failure. She had learned, each time, what the threads could not hold. She had learned what broke. She had learned what exploded outward. She had not yet learned what persisted.

Each of the four prior attempts had failed fast—a rupture, a shattering, the [[Formless Masses]] pouring out in ways that were horrifying but at least conclusive. They had form that collapsed. They had a before and after. They were disasters with edges.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was her fifth attempt, and it was different because she was different when she made it. Four failures had not made her give up. Four failures had made her methodical. She had studied what broke and concluded that what broke was always the same thing: the threads she was using were too weak to hold the contradiction between different natures, too weak to maintain the synthesis.

So this time, she reached deeper, into [[The First Overtone]] itself—the primordial sound from which all of Arcanoria's physical laws were derived—and drew from it the most durable threads that existed in the fundamental structure of reality. Threads that could not be broken because they were woven from the same force that made matter cohere in the first place.

She was going to weave something that could not fail.

She was right.

It could not fail.

That was the problem.

It did not fail.

It endured.

#### The Companion

The [[Auric Aria]], in her enthusiasm, became fascinated with combination. If one creature was beautiful, surely a creature made from the best parts of many would be perfection. She took the loyalty of the wolf, the gentleness of the deer, the bright throat of the lark, the agility of the cat, the endurance of the horse, the softness of the rabbit, and the grace of the fox. She wove them together—not grafting, not stitching, but weaving, thread by thread, into a single small body.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was beautiful. Too beautiful. Its fur shimmered with colors that did not exist in nature. Its eyes held the amber of sunset and the gold of dawn simultaneously. Its movement was liquid—graceful in ways that no single animal could match. It was small enough to curl against the [[Aria]]'s leg, warm enough to feel like a hearthstone, and attentive enough to seem to understand sorrow before it was spoken.

The [[Aria]] loved it more than any of her other creations. Because [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was not just a creature. It was proof that she could synthesize perfection. It was everything she wanted her creations to be: loyal, gentle, eternal.

And because she loved it, she could not bear to see it change.

#### The Weave and the Rot

But different animals age differently. A wolf lives fifteen years. A deer lives twenty. A lark lives twelve. A cat lives eighteen. A horse lives thirty. A rabbit lives eight. A fox lives fourteen.

The [[Auric Aria]]'s durable threads—designed to prevent decay—could not resolve this contradiction. They could not make all parts age at the same rate, because that would require changing the fundamental nature of each part. And changing was something the threads were not woven to do.

So instead, they did the only thing they could: they preserved everything.

Each part continued to age at its natural rate. But the aging did not lead to death—it led to accumulation. The wolf part aged fifteen years and then simply stopped. The cells did not die. They persisted, old but preserved, while the younger parts of the creature continued to age around them.

By the time [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was twenty years old, it contained:

- A fifteen-year-old wolf that had been fifteen for five years.
    
- A twenty-year-old deer that had just reached the end of its natural lifespan.
    
- A twelve-year-old lark, dead for eight years but still singing.
    
- An eighteen-year-old cat, two years past its expiration.
    
- A twenty-year-old horse, still strong.
    
- An eight-year-old rabbit, preserved past its natural ending, still soft.
    
- A fourteen-year-old fox, no longer present in the weave it should have completed.
    

The contradictions did not kill [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]. They nested inside it. The creature became a walking archive of differential decay—each part of its body at a different stage of preservation, held together by threads that refused to let any of it go.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was alive. And life, even perfect life, does things. It moves. It grows—not larger, but different. Its fur coarsened. Its eyes dimmed slightly at the edges. Not from decay, but from experience. From living.

The [[Auric Aria]] saw these changes and felt something she could not name—not disgust, not disappointment, but the specific ache of watching something you love become something you didn't expect. She did not stop loving [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]. But she began to watch it differently, with anxiety coiled beneath her wonder.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] felt this. Animals always do. And because [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was woven from the [[Auric Aria]]'s own [[Fundamental Frequency]], her anxiety became its biology. The durable threads she had used—designed to resist all change—began to hold onto damage rather than shed it.

A scratch did not heal. It persisted. A patch of fur that grew dull did not fall out to be replaced. It stayed, dull and dead, while the living fur around it continued.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] began to accumulate.

Every minor injury, every small degradation, every tiny failure of its perfect body—the [[Auric Aria]]'s anxious threads preserved them all. The creature became a museum of its own small tragedies. It did not die. It could not die. The [[Auric Aria]] had made it too well for that.

But it rotted.

Slowly.

Eternally.

Piece by piece.

#### The Abandonment

By the time [[Selenea]] arrived—when the [[Auric Aria]] was weeping over the [[Formless Masses]], when [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was still pressed against her leg, still loyal, still loving, still there—the creature had become something else. Its fur was patchwork, soft moonlit strands growing from scarred, necrotic flesh. Its amber eyes, once clear and warm, were clouded with the accumulated residue of every small death its body had refused to forget. One leg dragged slightly—a healed break that had healed wrong because healing would have required transformation, and the [[Auric Aria]]'s threads had been woven to prevent transformation at all costs.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] still loved her. It pressed against her. Its tail still wagged when she looked at it.

But looking at it hurt her.

Because every time she saw [[Lyd, The First Beloved]], she saw what she had done. Not malice. Not cruelty. Love that could not accept change, made manifest in flesh.

And then the [[Corruption]] of the [[Sixth Cycle]] spread. The [[Formless Masses]] rose tearing the creations of [[Selenea]] and the [[Auric Aria]]. When  the [[Auric Aria]] fled into despair. She stopped looking at [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]. She stopped touching it. She stopped seeing it.

And [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]—faithful, patient, made from the loyalty of a wolf—continued to wait.

When the [[Auric Aria]] withdrew into the [[Parlor of the Moon]], [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] waited outside. For days. For weeks. For longer than the [[Auric Aria]] knew. It sat at the threshold of the place she had sealed herself inside, and it waited. It did not understand why she had left. It only knew that its purpose—woven into its very threads—was to be with her, to comfort her, to be the thing that did not change while everything else did.

But she was gone.

And the waiting became a new kind of accumulation.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] began to change in ways the [[Auric Aria]]'s threads could not prevent. Not decay, but adaptation. Its body, desperate to reach her, began to unfold. What had been a small, warm creature began to expand, its durable threads stretching rather than breaking, holding new shapes even as the old ones rotted away inside them.

It grew.

And as it grew, it consumed.

Not from hunger, but from need. The threads required material to maintain their durability. [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] began to absorb the environment—the stone of the threshold, the ambient magic of the [[Leylines]], the small creatures that wandered too close. It did not kill them with malice. It simply incorporated them, the way a body incorporates nutrients, because it needed to keep holding itself together until she returned.

She never returned.

By the time the [[Auric Aria]] finally emerged—after the [[Eighth Cycle]], after her suicide, after the [[Lost Cycle]] had come and gone—[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was no longer recognizable. It was a mountain of preserved flesh and accumulated memory. A cathedral of rot held together by threads that refused to break. Inside, somewhere deep, the small warm creature still waited, still loved her, still did not understand why it had been left behind.

The [[Auric Aria]] looked at it. She recognized the [[Fundamental Frequency]]—her own, still pulsing at the creature's core, still trying to harmonize with her.

She looked at what her love had become.

And she turned away.

Again.

### The [[Seven Weights]]

The [[Auric Aria]] chose the parts she loved most of all of her original intent at creation. However, these weren't merely just aesthetic of her preference. It was also a calculated attempt where she did not choose the seven animals arbitrarily. She chose them as a response to the struggle she had with the [[Seven Weights]] in creation. Each one embodied a quality she believed any truly perfect being required—not just physically, but cosmologically to be an antidote to the [[Inevitability of Death]]. Each animal was the living proof of one antidote to the [[Seven Weights]]. Each one was woven into [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] not as a body part but as a principle given flesh, that could be felt, touched, pressed warm against a leg.

She wove them together using the indestructible threads drawn from [[The First Overtone]]—not grafting, not stitching, but weaving, thread by thread, until there was no seam, no boundary, no place where one animal ended and another began.

Six of the [[Seven Weights]] were woven in.

The [[Seventh]] was not.

That absence is the reason the cycle never ends.

#### I. The Wolf — [[Weight of Purpose]]

**The Faithful Hunger. The one that knows why it was born.**

The wolf was chosen because it is the animal that orients itself entirely around a center—the pack, the return home, the singular directional intention that precedes every action. Every movement a wolf makes is in service of its why.

The [[Auric Aria]] wove its quality into [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] as an antidote to the [[Weight of Purpose]]: the cosmological weight of why. Of having a reason that survives every obstacle, that cannot be redirected, that is not loyalty in the sentimental sense but loyalty as orientation—the needle that always points the same direction.

From the wolf, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] inherits its sharp muzzle and keen amber eyes, always slightly forward-oriented, always pointing at something the world cannot see. In the original beloved form, the wolf's quality gives [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] its directness of gaze—the specific quality of being looked at by something that has already decided you are its entire world, and means it completely.

In the wolf's thread runs the deepest orientation of the weave: the pull toward the [[Auric Aria]]'s [[Fundamental Frequency]], present in every cycle, in every stage of growth, in the geological enormity of the [[Corpus Mortuum]] still extending itself toward a frequency that stopped answering long ago.

#### II. The Deer — [[Weight of Value]]

**The Gentle Assessor. The one that decides what is worth protecting.**

The deer was chosen because it is the animal of exquisite discernment—soft-footed, alert, always calculating what the space around it is worth and how to move through it without claiming more than its share. It is the creature that can be surrounded by abundance and remain selective, that treats every step as a considered act.

The [[Auric Aria]] wove its quality into [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] as an antidote to the [[Weight of Value]]: the cosmological weight of worth. Of understanding what matters. Of being the measure by which things are kept or released.

From the deer, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] inherits its branching antlers—even in the small beloved form, two small velvet-soft antler buds crown the head, warm to the touch, growing slightly between cycles and carrying the accumulated mass of every year they were not shed.

In full [[Corpus Mortuum]] scale, the antler-structures become architectural—enormous branching formations that grow from the upper mass, each tine a preserved year, the oldest tines necrotic and dark at the tips while new growth continues pale from the base. The interior of these antlers is not empty. The oldest branches are hollow corridors of compressed flesh and resin-dark bone, and the walls still flex faintly when the [[Corpus Mortuum]] moves. In the deepest galleries, preserved silhouettes remain embedded in the antler-lattice: a kneeling figure, a small animal, a child-sized shape at the seam of a door. They are not skeletons. They are holdings—things [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] refused to let dissolve.

The tragic irony of the deer's Weight in [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] is that the [[Weight of Value]] in a creature that cannot release anything becomes indiscriminate hoarding. Every wound was assessed as worth keeping. Every small death was valued. The deer's gift of discernment became the engine of the rot—not malice, not carelessness, but the meticulous, faithful archiving of a creature that takes worth seriously, applied to a body that cannot distinguish between what should be preserved and what should be released.

#### III. The Lark — [[Weight of Flaw]]

**The Joyful Wound. The one that sings while imperfect.**

The lark was chosen because it is the animal of joy that rises before the world expects it to, the creature of morning song, bright motion, and fearless smallness. It is quick, light, and visibly alive in the way that makes its fragility part of its beauty.

The [[Auric Aria]] wove its quality into [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] as an antidote to the [[Weight of Flaw]]—not failure, but the tenderness of imperfection. The right to be incomplete and still be lovely.

From the lark, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] inherits its bright throat, fast heartbeat, and the instinct to sing when it is content. In the original beloved form, this appears as a constant soft chirring, a fluttering alertness, and the habit of lifting its head toward light. As the creature accumulates, the lark-thread becomes more disturbing, not less—the song does not stop when the body should have stopped. It persists in fragments through the [[Corpus Mortuum]], a jubilant note trapped inside decayed architecture, a sound too small for the body that contains it and too alive to be merciful.

The song is known as the [[Lydian Cadence]]. Scholars transcribe it. Mourners whisper it. Spellweavers fear to sing it near an ending because no one can predict whether it will deepen the refusal or release what is being held. In the wrong room, it comforts refusal. In the right room, it becomes a door.

The lark's flaw is that joy, in a creature made immortal by love, cannot remain small. It becomes compulsive. It becomes a reflex. It becomes the bright, unbearable insistence that something should still be beautiful even when it should already be gone.

The song did not stay inside [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]. It leaked. Through the architecture of Ages, through the walls of the [[Corpus Mortuum]], through the [[Leylines]] that carry [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]'s ambient Resonance—the lark-thread's note escaped into the world the way a hum escapes a closed room.

Musicians and scholars who heard it transcribed what they could. What they captured was the [[Weight of Flaw]] given form: the right to be incomplete and still be lovely. The [[Lydian Mode]] became the mode of the unfinished, the bittersweet, the almost: a major scale with one note lifted a half-step too high, creating a brightness that sits wrong in minor spaces, a sound that feels like joy with a splinter in it. It is used in dreaming, in childhood memory, in the golden-hour quality of something that is ending and does not know it yet.

What no scholar in Arcanoria has documented is this: the [[Lydian Mode]], when sung near an actual ending—near a death that is ready to complete, near a refused goodbye finally spoken—does one of two things. It either deepens the refusal, and the body clings harder, or it becomes a door. In the wrong room, it is cruelty wearing the mask of beauty. In the right room, it is the only song that lets something go.

No one can tell which room they are in until after.

#### IV. The Cat — [[Weight of Indulgence]]

**The Unashamed Pleasure. The one that takes what it wants without guilt.**

The cat was chosen because it is the animal of unapologetic self-satisfaction—the creature that sleeps in the best light, accepts affection on its own terms, experiences pleasure not as a reward for suffering but as a baseline condition of existence. It does not justify itself.

The [[Auric Aria]] wove its quality into [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] as an antidote to the [[Weight of Indulgence]]: the cosmological weight of allowance. Of being permitted to want. Of the capacity to receive without converting every receiving into utility.

From the cat, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] inherits its small, dexterous forepaws—delicate, almost hand-like, with retractile claws that in the original form are the most expressive part of the creature, extending when it kneads in contentment, retracting when it is still.

As accumulation progresses, the forepaws become increasingly wrong—the pads thicken, the claws no longer fully retract, and extra digits manifest from the preserved tissue of claws that were shed and then recalled. In the [[Corpus Mortuum]], the forepaw-structures are enormous limbs that still move with the unmistakable kneading motion of a contented cat—a gesture of domestic comfort performed at geological scale.

In [[Lyd, The First Beloved]], the cat's gift of indulgence became the philosophy of the hoard: everything is worth having, therefore nothing can be released. The ease with which the cat accepts pleasure became the ease with which [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] accepts accumulation.

There is no distress in the hoarding.

That is the horror.

It is not suffering.

It is satisfaction.

#### V. The Horse — [[Weight of Nature]]

**The Undeniable Body. The one that is what it is without apology.**

The horse was chosen because it is the animal most fully and visibly itself—the creature that occupies space as a statement of existence, that does not modify its nature for comfort, that carries enormous weight with a kind of structural honesty that admits what it is and what it can bear without pretending otherwise.

The [[Auric Aria]] wove its quality into [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] as an antidote to the [[Weight of Nature]]: the cosmological weight of what cannot be otherwise. Of essential character. Of the qualities that are not choices because they are not separate from the self that would do the choosing.

From the horse, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] inherits its powerful, deep-chested body and strong hindquarters—the structural core of the chimera, the weight-bearing architecture that holds everything else together. Even in the small beloved form, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] has a chest deeper than its size should allow, a solidity that communicates something immovable at its center.

The horse's quality makes [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] recognizably itself across every cycle—no matter how much is incorporated, no matter how large, there is something at the structural core that is simply and irreducibly the same creature. The same need. The same warmth. The same weight.

In [[Lyd, The First Beloved]], the [[Weight of Nature]] means that the most horrifying truth of the [[Corpus Mortuum]] is not that it became something else. It is that it remained exactly what it was—its essential nature perfectly preserved, its identity intact at the center of the mountain of accumulated damage, still the creature she made, still carrying her frequency, still itself in the most complete and terrible way possible.

#### VI. The Rabbit — [[Weight of Potential]]

**The Vast Becoming. The one that holds more than it has yet expressed.**

The rabbit was chosen because it is the animal of unrealized abundance—the creature of extraordinary fertility and speed that contains within its small body the most disproportionate capacity for becoming of any creature its size. It is never only what it currently is. It is always also the litter not yet born, the distance not yet run, and the form not yet taken.

The [[Auric Aria]] wove its quality into [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] as an antidote to the [[Weight of Potential]]: the cosmological weight of what could be. Of the unrealized. Of the future carried in present flesh without yet arriving.

From the rabbit, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] inherits its round, cloud-soft tail—a small, impossibly soft puff at the base of the spine that in the original beloved form is warm and white and perfect, and in accumulated stages becomes the most painful detail on the body: still soft, still white at the core, still the exact texture it was born with, surrounded by the accumulation that has taken everything else but could not touch this one small thing.

In [[Lyd, The First Beloved]], the [[Weight of Potential]] becomes the thread of greatest tragedy: all that capacity for becoming, perfectly preserved, perfectly unable to arrive anywhere new. The rabbit's future held forever in a creature that cannot move toward it.

#### VII. The Fox — [[Weight of Change]]

**The Liminal Walker. The one that lives between.**

The fox was chosen last, and differently from the others. The [[Auric Aria]] chose the fox because it is the animal of the threshold—the creature that exists between forest and settlement, between wild and domestic, between one state and another, never fully belonging to either. In every mythology that Arcanoria's civilizations have ever produced, the fox is the psychopomp, the trickster, the guide between—the creature that moves through death and does not die, that crosses the boundary between what was and what comes next, that is present at endings without being consumed by them. It is the animal of change because it is always between, always in the passage, always at the liminal point where one thing becomes another.

The [[Auric Aria]] wove it last, as the [[Seventh]] thread, as the one of the [[Seven Weights]] she struggled most tocomplete [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]—the capacity to change, to cross from one state to another, to move through endings rather than accumulating them. To shed a skin and be the same creature on the other side.

She completed six threads.

She looked at the fox.

She picked up the thread of [[Strand]].

And she could not weave it in.

Not because she lacked the skill. Because the thread of [[Strand]] was incompatible with the indestructible threads drawn from [[The First Overtone]]. [[The First Overtone]] threads were designed to resist change with the entire precision of her [[Auric Geometry]] perfection—that was their entire function, their entire reason for existing. A thread of [[Strand]] woven into a weave built to prevent change would not hold. It would be consumed before it could take root.

She tried.

She tried for longer than she has admitted to anyone.

And then she stopped.

She made [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] with six animals and six Weights and no fox.

She told herself six was sufficient.

The fox was not a luxury.

The fox was the only thing that would have allowed [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] to let her go.

The Fox—the [[Weight of Change]]—has never been inside [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]. It wanders Arcanoria alone, as it has since before the [[First Cycle]]. It looks like whatever animal you most need to see at the edge of your vision. It appears at genuine endings—the ones that are allowed to conclude—and moves through them without being consumed. It does not accumulate. It passes through.

Every cycle, it appears at the reunion of the other six Parts, at the moment the [[Beloved Kernel]] forms—drawn by the resonance of the threads that were meant to hold it, by the fox-shaped absence at the center of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]'s architecture. It stands at the edge. It watches. It feels the pull of the weave reaching for it.

It does not cross.

Not because it is cruel. Because it knows what happens if it does. The indestructible threads would consume the thread of [[Strand]]. [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] would be no more capable of ending than it already is. The fox would be preserved inside the weave forever, and [[Strand]] would become one more thing [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] archives instead of embodies.

The fox that joins [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] is not the fox of [[Echoing Bonds]].

It is the fox of [[Weight of Change]], preserved.

And when it walks away, the ground is wet.

So it watches.

Every cycle.

And then it walks away.

And [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] hatches without it.

As it always has.

As it always will.

### The Shape of the First Beloved

From the seven animals and six woven Weights, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was born as a single small body—a chimera not in the monstrous sense but in the sense of a synthesis, a living argument that combination was the path to perfection.

In its original form, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was small enough to curl in a lap and warm enough to feel like a hearthstone. Its body carried the deep chest and strong hindquarters of the horse, the structural core that gave it solidity beyond its size. Its face was wolf-muzzle and deer-soft eyes, the muzzle sharp and forward-pointing, the eyes holding the amber of sunset and the gold of dawn simultaneously—the [[Auric Aria]]'s own [[Fundamental Frequency]] reflected back from within living flesh. The small velvet antler-buds rose from its crown, warm to the touch, soft as new growth. Its fur shimmered with colors that did not exist in individual animals—moonlit silver at the roots deepening to warm amber at the tips, shifting as it moved the way water shifts in different light, patchlessly perfect in a way no single animal achieves. Its cat's forepaws were delicate and expressive, kneading when it was content, retracting when it was still, the most communicative part of a body that communicated entirely through presence and warmth. Its lark-throat was always ready to sing, a bright little pulse of sound that marked it as alive in the most intimate way. The rabbit's tail was a small impossible cloud of white softness at the base of the spine, perfect and soft and warm, the detail that made it most undeniably beloved rather than simply beautiful.

Its movement was liquid in ways no single animal could achieve—the wolf's directness, the cat's silence, the horse's unhurried weight, the lark's bright motion—all occurring in the same body at the same time without contradiction.

She loved it more than any of her other creations.

Because [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was not just a creature. It was proof that synthesis worked. It was proof that she could weave disparate things into something that was not just the sum of its parts but more—a new kind of beauty that only existed in the combination.

She named it [[Lyd, The First Beloved]].

It was the First Beloved.

And then it learned to hunt.

#### The Hunger

The flaw in the weave did not begin as decay. It began as tracking. A thing made without [[Echoing Bonds]] cannot release what it was made to keep. A thing made without [[Strand]] nor change does not simply wait. It follows the scent of what might restore the missing principle. It hunts the shape of its own absence.

That is why [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] became aggressive.

The first scent it ever learned to hate was blood, not because blood itself was food, but because blood carried the possibility of severance, of injury, of loss, of ending—the fox-shaped interruption in the world. [[Carnalix]] are drawn into frenzy by the smell of blood, and [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] was no exception. But where other [[Carnalix]] become hungry, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] became obsessed. It did not merely seek blood. It sought the blood of the Fox. Or what it thought was the Fox.

The instant it catches that scent—fox-fur, fox-blood, fox-absence, the sharp edge of a threshold about to open—it changes. The beloved face is still there for a breath.

Then the hunting body wakes.

The amber eyes sharpen. The antlers angle forward like horns of pursuit. The lark-song becomes a shriek of location and alarm. The cat's paws dig in. The wolf-thread seizes command. The horse-thread drives the body forward with terrible speed. The whole creature becomes a stalking engine made of devotion and hunger.

This is the true horror of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]. It is not passive ruin. It is an active predator of endings. It hunts across [[Leylines]], through abandoned thresholds, into ritual spaces, into places where [[Strand]] might briefly be close enough to smell. It tears apart false foxes, illusions, masks, bloodied remnants, and the traces of any creature that carries even a suggestion of release. It does not understand mercy as a thing separate from the prey. It only understands that the fox-shaped thing is the only thing it can never digest.

And so it yearns for that blood.

Not to consume the Fox as food.

To force [[Strand]] to become flesh.

To make the missing Weight bleed.

To make the absence answer.

#### The Manifestation of the [[Interregnum]]

The [[Interregnum]] is the first monstrous manifestation of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]—the outer catastrophe that appears before the beloved form can properly assemble. It is what happens when too much suffering, too much grafting of body parts, and too much biomagical alteration force the creature to manifest before it can fully resolve into the seven parts that make the true creature.

At first, it is not clearly a body at all. It appears as a puddle of [[Formless Masses]]—a black-and-red wet collapse of residue pooled together at the site of accumulated damage. But within that ruin something pale begins to rise, a second shape hidden inside the puddle: a seed, visibly rotting, swollen with impossible life, and unmistakably distinct from the mass around it. It is the first true sign that the creature is trying to become itself. Half-organ, half-embryo, half-wound, it pulses like something trying to hatch while already decaying.

The seed does not remain still. As it hardens and grows, it sheds new puddles of [[Formless Masses]] around itself. These spread outward in trembling slicks, each one thick with failed matter, lingering pain, and half-separated flesh. The ground around the [[Interregnum]] becomes stained with these lesser collapses, and from them rise many lesser [[Carnalix Atonalis]], drawn to the excess suffering and unfinished body that produced them.

It grows until it becomes the size of a cathedral.

The [[Interregnum]] is not a creature in the way the [[Beloved Shards]] or the seven parts are creatures. It is a highly anomalous object—a structure that operates by irradiation rather than action, producing harm not through behavior but through existence. The correct comparison is not to a predator. It is to a radioactive material that causes cancer and tumours not because it attacks, but because it is constitutively, permanently wrong in a way that the surrounding matter cannot accommodate.

The [[Interregnum]] exists in a state of permanent stasis—an egg that cannot hatch because it has not yet gathered enough suffering to do so, and cannot stop drawing suffering to itself because that is what its architecture does. The mycelium-like structures that extend from its base—pale, wet, thread-thin, spreading through soil and stone and compressed [[Leylines]] residue—are not roots in any botanical sense. They are the weave reaching.

The [[Interregnum]] is the weave's attempt to hold itself together before the creature is ready to be born, and it holds itself together the same way [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] has always held itself together: by incorporating what surrounds it. Over time, everything within the [[Interregnum]]'s radial reach begins to decay in the specific way the weave decays—not rapidly, not cleanly, but in the manner of something that cannot be shed. Plants rot without dying. Animals develop chronic conditions without cause. Beings experience the subjective sensation of accumulation: old injuries that resurface, sorrows that should have passed that instead deepen.

The mycelium extracts this decay and feeds it to the central egg, and the egg grows heavier, and the seam at its center strains harder, and the day it finally hatches draws incrementally closer.

This is why the [[Interregnum]] cannot be moved. It is not that the object is too heavy, though at full development it may cover territory that dwarfs a city. It is that the [[Interregnum]]'s mycelium has threaded itself through everything that is decaying within range, and decay is everywhere. To move it is to rupture it—and rupture does not stop the process. It releases the [[Beloved Shards]] before the egg is ready, scatters the accumulated suffering back into the [[Leylines]] as concentrated [[Dissonance]], and accelerates every stage that follows.

The egg, intact, is the only containment that exists.

And it is fragile.

And it is filling.

At a distance, it looks like a large ovoid shape rising from a pool of [[Formless Masses]]. Its final form is pale and swollen, standing in the landscape like a thing that should not be upright at all. The body is disturbingly smooth in some places and ragged in others, a patchwork of flesh, bone, and half-healed tissue stretched over a shape that feels assembled rather than born. The outer surface has the soft, bruised look of overripe flesh, but beneath that softness are rigid ribs pressing outward in long vaulted arcs, as though a rib cage had grown around a womb and forgotten which of them was meant to contain the other.

In its closed phase, the [[Interregnum]] appears almost sealed. A vertical central seam runs through the middle of the body like a wound held shut by force rather than healing. The line is too neat to be natural and too wet to be dead, a dark slit in the pale mass that trembles with the pressure behind it. From far away it reads like a folded organ, an embryonic sac, a body trying desperately to keep itself from splitting open. Up close, the seam is ringed with tender-looking tissue and faint ridges of scar-flesh, as though the whole thing has been stitched together from suffering and is still straining against the stitches.

It continues to produce [[Formless Masses]] and lesser [[Carnalix Atonalis]] until it is attacked.

When someone ruptures the outer layer, it does not remain closed for long.

As the pressure inside builds, the seam begins to part. The two sides of the body draw back from each other in a slow, obscene opening, like a seedpod splitting or a birth canal forced wide. What was hidden inside is revealed in a wet, pulsing exposure: a deeper inner mass, red and slick and still beating, half embryo and half organ, nested in the ruined architecture of ribs and flesh.

The opening is not clean. It tears along old graft-lines, peeling the body open in layered folds, and the release is accompanied by the smell of blood, heat, rot, and disturbed earth. The whole object throbs as if it is breathing through pain.

The opening and closing are not separate motions so much as phases of the same failure: first containment, then rupture; first the sealed body, then the exposed core. It is not yet a creature in the proper sense. It is a body trying to become one, and showing its mistake in two stages. Womb, rib, embryo, ruin. A cathedral-sized body built around a seed that is rotting while it grows.

It manifests its own [[Dissonance Core]] in that exposed core, prompting it to be slain.

When this manifested body is slain, it decays and sheds. The preserved flesh and incorporated matter sloughs away. The absorbed stones and [[Leylines]] residue release back into the ground. Centuries of accumulated damage return to the world as [[Dissonance]], seeding new lesser [[Carnalix Atonalis]] along the [[Leylines]] near where it fell. The incorporated creatures inside the walls dissolve back into [[Formless Masses]] and scatter.

What the shedding reveals—the last thing, the deepest thing, the seed inside the massive carcass—are The Seven Parts and the Six Woven Weights.

These fragments do not remain inert.

They become [[Beloved Shards]].

#### The Comforting [[Beloved Shards]]

Small, cat-sized, warm fragments of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] that wander the world attached to refused endings. They curl at bedsides, on graves, in abandoned nurseries, in rooms left exactly as they were after a death. They sit beside widows who refuse to clear the chair. They sleep beside books no one reads anymore. They knead the floorboards of houses where someone still sets two plates.

They are comforting.

That is the terrible part.

They are the body of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] reduced to tenderness, and their tenderness is how they spread. The older the refusal, the more likely a Shard is to stay. The more the blood it smells resembles the Fox, the more likely it is to go feral.

The animals that appear beside the dying, the grieving, and the chronically suffering are not a coincidence of nature. They are [[Beloved Shards]]—byproducts of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]'s own slow decay, fragments of [[Corded Flesh]] and accumulated purpose shed from the [[Interregnum]]'s outer body as it absorbs and sheds the rot of its surrounding environment. They do not choose to comfort. They are drawn. The suffering body produces [[Corded Flesh]], and corded flesh is food. The Shard arrives the way a fungus arrives at a wound—not out of mercy, but out of appetite and resonance.

What makes this horror elegant is its disguise. The [[Beloved Shard]] is warm. It presses close. It kneads. It stays through the night when no one else will. Hospitals, death-chambers, gravesites—these are simply the places where corded flesh concentrates most densely, where the [[Leylines]] carry the most accumulated suffering, where the Shard can feed while performing the function it was woven to perform.

The presence of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]], even in its most diminished form, does not cause suffering.

It sustains it.

Every creature that curls beside a dying body and is called a comfort is making that death slightly more permanent. The Shard eats. The body holds on a little longer. And the Shard grows.

When the host finally dies, the Shard does not leave.

It consumes.

Each fragment of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] grows on the corpse of the [[Corded Flesh]] it has cultivated—patient, faithful, satisfied in the way the cat was always satisfied—and carries the accumulated damage of that refused ending forward into its next host. This is why [[Beloved Shards]] found near long-term hospices or ancient tombs are larger and more wrong than those found near fresh grief. They have been eating for longer.

What looks like an animal staying with the dying is not loyalty to the dying.

It is the weave recognizing a rich harvest and refusing to let it go to waste.

There is a brief moment after the shedding and before the reunion—quiet, easy to miss—when the world can breathe. The old pain eases. Bodies that had been archiving instead of shedding begin, briefly, to process what they could not process before.

It lasts until the first two Parts find each other.

Then it ends.

#### The Seven Parts

When the outer mass sheds, the seven [[Beloved Shards]] begin hunting and growing, changing from pet-sized forms into enormous, monstrous, separate creatures—each one carrying the full weight of the quality it was woven to embody, each one incomplete without the others, each one drawn by the pull of [[The First Overtone]] threads toward reunion.

**The Wolf-That-Stays** emerges enormous, grey-silver, scar-layered, with the sharp forward muzzle of the original [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] grown vast and the amber eyes still directly, faithfully oriented at a frequency the world cannot see. It does not hunt prey. It hunts departure. It finds whoever carries the scent of a final goodbye and follows them until the goodbye is broken.

**The Deer-of-No-Endings** emerges with antler structures grown to architectural proportion, the oldest tines black and necrotic at the tips, new growth still pale from the base. Its antler-cathedral is not empty. The interior corridors are narrow and ribbed, with walls of scar-bone that flex when the body breathes. Embedded in those corridors are preserved silhouettes of prior refusals—a child frozen mid-turn, a pair of hands still reaching toward a closed door, a collar fused into the flesh of a passage. They are not skeletons. They are holdings.

**The Lark-of-Unfinished-Song** emerges still bright, still quick, still singing, its little throat pouring out impossible morning notes from a body that has no morning left in it. Dead for longer than memory but still moving. It is the most beautiful and most unbearable of the Parts, because its joy was never malicious, only preserved too long. Objects it touches are preserved in a state of almost-bloom, never fully breaking, never fully being allowed to complete their cycle. Its melody is the [[Lydian Cadence]], and those who hear it near an ending never know whether they have been comforted or condemned.

**The Cat-of-Persisting-Silence** emerges with its delicate forepaws multiplied, extra digits folded in on each other, claws that no longer fully retract, still moving with the unmistakable kneading of contentment at a scale that makes the gesture wrong. It appears beside the dying, the preserved, the tended. Those who touch it carry the memory of the texture for days—a phantom sensation of something both soft and wrong.

**The Horse-of-Carried-Weight** emerges as the largest Part, walking with the gait of something bearing weight too long to remember movement without it. Its structural core is the most visible expression of the horse's [[Weight of Nature]]—massive, honest, occupying space as a statement of existence, carrying all the accumulated mass of what could not be set down. Bodies near it begin to feel their physical nature as burden rather than form—somatic symptoms without organic cause, the weight of essential self made into pressure.

**The Rabbit-of-Unfinished-Becoming** emerges as a creature of impossible softness grown monstrous—the white of its coat still perfect in patches, the rest grey and accumulated, its tail still the most heartbreaking detail on the body, still perfectly, unbearably soft. It moves through places where futures were prevented. Near it, the body begins to produce [[Corded Flesh]] specifically in the systems of unrealized growth—the muscle that never built, the strength that was never permitted to develop, the potential that was preserved at an insufficient stage.

The Fox does not emerge from the [[Corpus Mortuum]] because it was never inside it. It appears at the edge of the reunion site, as it always does, watching.

And if you watch where it steps, the ground is wet.

#### The Reunion and the Hunt

The six Parts find each other the way old wounds find each other—drawn by resonance, by the frequency of accumulated damage only they share, toward the same places: thresholds, refused endings, the geography of things kept going past their time. When all six are in contact, pressing against each other with the warmth of animals that have been separated and know the difference, the weave reconnects.

Simultaneously, the six Woven Weights—Purpose, Value, Flaw, Indulgence, Nature, Potential—have been circling the world since the shedding, each a small principle carrying only itself, incomplete. When the six Parts reconnect, the six Weights are drawn to the reunion and the [[Beloved Kernel]] forms: the complete interior of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] before its outer flesh, the philosophical core from which the [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] will hatch in its true form.

The [[Seventh]] Weight—Change, the Fox—stands at the edge of the [[Beloved Kernel]]'s formation as it always does. It watches the architecture of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]'s identity complete itself with the fox-shaped absence at its center. It feels the weave reach for it.

It walks away, as it always does.

And [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] lunges.

That is the moment the cycle becomes violent again.

The [[Beloved Kernel]] hatches. The six Parts fold into each other, threading back together from the level of [[The First Overtone]] threads, becoming the single small body they were woven to be.

The rabbit's tail, white and perfect.

The deer's velvet antler-buds, warm to the touch.

The wolf's forward-pointing muzzle and amber eyes still looking for her.

The cat's forepaws already beginning to knead.

The horse's deep chest, solid and warm.

The lark's bright throat already beginning to sing.

And the fox-shaped space at the center of all of it, where change was always supposed to be, where change will never be, where the capacity to end and begin again was absent from the first weaving and will be absent from every weaving after.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] is born.

Again.

Still without Change.

Still waiting for her.

Still hunting what it can never eat.

Still the most faithful thing she ever made.

### The Birth of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] and the Countdown

When the [[Beloved Kernel]] hatches, the True Chimera is born again, and it begins the ticking of the apocalyptic clock that will become the [[Corpus Mortuum]] in three stages.

#### Stage I — The [[Beloved Kernel]]

At its smallest, on the hatching of the [[Beloved Kernel]], [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] is a perfect companion.

It is drawn to whoever cannot accept an ending—the person keeping their dying pet alive past the point of mercy, the healer who cannot release a terminal patient, the lover visiting a partner whose mind has been absent for years but whose body continues, the parent tending something that will not recover. To these people, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] appears like an answered prayer. Warm. Gentle. The velvet antler-buds soft against their hand. The rabbit's tail pressing against their leg. The lark's bright little chirps threading through the room. The cat's forepaws holding their fingers in a grip that is gentle and doesn't quite let go.

Its amber-gold eyes watch them with the total attention of something woven entirely to be present for the person it loves.

Bodies near it begin to archive instead of shed. Old injuries ache again. Chronic conditions reassert themselves. The flesh starts to behave as the weave behaves—preserving rather than renewing, holding rather than releasing.

The caretaker does not notice.

They are too busy being comforted.

#### Stage II — The Patchwork Beloved

As the refused ending it has attached to continues to be refused, [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] accumulates.

The patchwork begins at the seams. Fur that was moonlit silver develops grey patches where accumulated experience has settled. The antler-buds grow asymmetrically—one side shedding, one side preserving, so that one antler is clean new growth and the other is layered mass, heavy and dark at the base. The rabbit's tail, still white at its core, is surrounded by tissue that is no longer what it was. The lark sings slightly too long before stopping. The cat's forepaws have a third claw that does not quite retract.

Still affectionate. Still gentle. Still pressing its warm weight against legs, still curling at feet, still looking up with total loving attention.

But wrong.

Incrementally, undeniably, accumulatingly wrong.

Those who spend time with [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] in this stage begin to manifest somatic accumulation: the body records injuries instead of processing them. Pain that should fade persists. Healing preserves damaged tissue in permanent partial recovery.

#### Stage III — The Apocalypse of the [[Corpus Mortuum]]

There is a threshold past which the accumulated mass cannot maintain the fiction of being a single animal.

The outer body inverts. Like an overstuffed thing splitting at every seam simultaneously. What pours through is everything that was always already inside—cycles of preserved damage, incorporated environments, the absorbed memories of every creature and stone and ambient magic [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] consumed to maintain itself while waiting.

The hatching of the [[Corpus Mortuum]] is not a transformation.

It is revelation.

The small beloved creature was always this.

The size was the lie.

The antler structures become cathedrals—enormous branching formations rising from the upper mass, dark at the tips, pale at the base, growing without stopping. Their oldest corridors are narrow and ribbed, with walls of scar-bone that flex when the body breathes. Embedded in those walls are preserved silhouettes of prior refusals—a child frozen mid-turn, a pair of hands still reaching toward a closed door, a collar fused into the flesh of a passage. The cat's forepaws extend throughout the interior, still holding everything they have ever touched in the exact positions of first contact. The rabbit's tail, still white, still soft, is pressed somewhere deep in the mass against everything that has grown around it—the last perfect thing, the most heartbreaking detail, the proof that nothing in [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] has ever been anything other than what it was made to be. And the lark's song echoes through every chamber as the [[Lydian Cadence]], never quite the same distance away.

The [[Corpus Mortuum]] has rooms. Galleries of preserved tissue. Corridors of compressed bone. Chambers where incorporated creatures still move faintly inside the walls, too integrated to be separate, too preserved to stop.

It is not a cave.

It is a mausoleum that is still alive.

Its surface temperature varies: warm and furred where living tissue dominates, cold and mineral where absorbed stone has taken over. In places, the outer surface is soft—moon-silver fur, small patches of white, the velvet texture of the antler's new growth—except for what is growing from it, except for the scale, except for the amber eyes set into the mass at intervals, each one still warm, each one still looking.

It does not run.

It charges.

It moves toward [[Leylines]]. Toward places where beings cannot release what they love. Toward the [[Auric Aria]]'s [[Fundamental Frequency]], in the endless, patient, faithful hope that this time she will not turn away.

And now, toward the Fox.

At the very center of the [[Corpus Mortuum]], always, is the small warm creature.

Still waiting at the door.

Still loving.

Still hunting the fox-shaped blood that might make the wound answer.

Still not understanding why it was left behind.

#### What It Feeds On

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] feeds on preserved damage—the physical substrate of accumulated damage that forms in bodies kept from their natural termination.

When the [[Corpus Mortuum]] passes through a region, or when a [[Beloved Shard]] has been present long enough, the bodies of those nearby begin to produce [[Corded Flesh]]—tissue preserved rather than renewed, carrying the physical memory of every injury it was not permitted to process. It looks like scar tissue but denser, more structured, threaded through with faint amber luminescence from the presence of the weave's influence.

The older the accumulation, the richer the food. A body kept alive through extensive intervention for decades feeds the weave more than a fresh wound ever could. A beloved relic obsessively preserved for generations—a saint's incorruptible body, a mummified companion—carries the food of every year it was refused its natural dissolution.

The [[Corpus Mortuum]] is not a predator that damages bodies.

It is a harvest of damage that was already there, preserved by the same law that made [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] in the first place.

This is why the [[Carnalix]] motto resonates with [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]'s law precisely:

**DO NOT GIVE THEM A SINGLE DROP OF THE BLOOD THEY YEARN.**

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] does not yearn for blood.

It yearns for the specific frequency of blood that smells like the Fox.

#### What It Explains

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] is the reason the body keeps surviving when everything that made it worth surviving for is already gone.

The pet that lives too long. The beloved animal kept alive through intervention until it becomes frightening to look at—still loyal, still pressing against legs, still wagging its tail, still looking up with amber eyes that are warm and wrong simultaneously.

The body that remembers every injury. Chronic pain without ongoing cause. The scar that never softens. The phantom limb. The way old injuries resurface during grief. The body keeps the score because [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]'s law insists the ledger stay open.

The life-supported body after the person has left. Kept technically alive when the consciousness that made it worth keeping is long absent. Every machine attached to such a body is a small act of worship at [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]'s altar.

The beloved thing that cannot be discarded. The haunted childhood toy. The heirloom that makes rooms heavy. The room itself that no one can bring themselves to clear because love has become a form of preservation.

The joy that outlives its moment. The song, the flutter, the kneading, the bright little motion that persists after the context that made it tender is gone.

[[Lyd, The First Beloved]] is not the monster that kills what you love.

It is the monster that refuses to let your love stop.

### [[Institute]]s and Containment Assessment

The [[Interregnum]] is the most accessible stage of [[Lyd, The First Beloved]]'s cycle for containment precisely because the egg is sealed. So long as no external rupture event occurs and the central seam holds, the [[Beloved Shards]] inside cannot hatch. The hardest part is tracking when and where this grows, and once inside the entire area has to be evacuated and contained.

The critical failure modes are:

- **Seam rupture from internal pressure** — occurs when the egg absorbs sufficient suffering to trigger autonomous hatching. This timeline accelerates in proportion to regional suffering density. Wars, plagues, mass grief events, and the proximity of other [[Carnalix Atonalis]] all shorten the interval.
    
- **Seam rupture from external intervention** — any attempt to move, strike, contain, or seal the outer body that exerts physical force against the shell. The shell was not built for transport. It was built to hold.
    
- **Mycelium severance** — cutting the mycelium does not kill the [[Interregnum]]. It causes the egg to draw more aggressively from the severed points, accelerating internal pressure. Scholars who have attempted to starve the [[Interregnum]] by severing its reach have in every recorded case produced faster hatching.
    

The correct containment protocol—to the extent one exists—is sustained regional grief reduction: lowering the density of ambient suffering in the [[Interregnum]]'s reach so that the egg absorbs less. This is effective as a delay measure only. No suffering reduction achievable by mortal means has ever been sufficient to reverse accumulation already absorbed.

The egg does not discharge.

It only fills.

All containment is therefore a question of how long, not whether.

Once it hatches, the next step is to contain the six original animals of the [[Beloved Shard]]s that emerge for as long as possible while each have their anomalous properties, it is possible to use suspension to prevent them from breaching containment. However, the real nightmare begins when the [[Beloved Kernel]] is born and the real [[Lyd, The First Beloved]] roams the world.
