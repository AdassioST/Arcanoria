A [[Great Chronicler]] [[Spellweaver]] cursed by [[Primal White Noise]] containing the canonical knowledge and wiki of [[Arcanoria]]. The only survivor of the [[First Reset]] by finding a place beyond time as God's blind spot after escaping from the [[Cataclysmic Aftermath]] of the [[Polychord Crisis]] and the firing of the ultimate weapon.

His story is inspired by Galileo Galilei. He was pursuing knowledge of [[Cosmic Motion]] and what happened during the [[Library of Heaven's Tower]]. Came to the realization that there was something that infected every scientist mind which lead to their demise, a sort of noise intrusion in their thoughts that made them unable to communicate with each other rendering everyone a lunatic. He realized it had to do with the way the cosmos itself worked. To pursue the truth, he developed advanced [[Luminance]] [[Flux]] lens technology to observe the [[Moon]] and realize why it wasn't part of the scriptures of [[Cosmic Motion]]. 

When he was close to uncover the truth after realizing there was something living inside the [[Moon]] the [[Auric Aria]] smithed him. She wanted to prevent his theories of the models detailing the structure and the anatomy of the [[Moon]] containing something from spreading and causing another disaster like the [[Library of Heaven's Tower]]. For this, the [[Auric Aria]] created an artificial "sun" of [[Aetherlight]] in between the [[Moon]] his own lenses, weaponizing the tools he had developed in one great eclipse that would burn his eyes due to his search of knowledge and highly enhanced images.

The searing light rendered him permanently blind because of how focused the light became through his own spell, and the [[Auric Aria]] hoped that this was enough of a lesson so that he wouldn't be able to study the [[Moon]] ever again. However, the forced blindness eventually drove him to seek knowledge of the universe coming to the realization of what [[Primal White Noise]] is through creative work-arounds to "see without seeing." The new type of [[Imaginative]] "lenses" originated from the [[Fractal Kaleidoscope]] that the [[Auric Aria]] created out of remorse during the [[Seven Dark Fates]]. It is this very phenomenon that he adapted to become his own instrument of an apparatus that could be used for studying an even smaller fabric of the universe, the impossible lenses born from divine regret became known as "The Eye."

### The First Meeting on the [[Ballad]] of the White Clots

This is the first meeting of [[Civilization]] with [[The White-Touched Archivist]] during [[Ages]] 0 after one of the early tasks of clearing out the [[Dissonance]] nearby of the starting point of [[Civilization]]. #### Introduction

The last one dissolved on [[Legend]]'s blade like it had always been smoke pretending to have opinions.

The ruined cathedral exhaled around them. Vaulted ribs of blackened stone rose into open sky, the roof long since surrendered to whatever had happened here before the [[Great Fracture]]. 

Golden forest sprawled through the collapsed transept in every direction, canopy catching the last amber light of the Echo, luminous dust spiraling upward through the broken windows as though the cathedral were breathing it out. The floor was cracked flagstone and old root. The walls were still magnificent in the way ruins are magnificent — grandeur present in what remained, insisting on itself despite everything.

The [[Atonalis]] were gone. The [[Formless Masses]] were gone. What remained was the silence of a large space that had recently held violence and was now politely pretending otherwise — and the fissure in the far wall still bleeding white light, cold and sourceless against all that warm gold. At its base, a dozen white clots of static pooled in slow, bovine clusters, wrong in the specific way a note played a half-tone too high is wrong.

[[Legend]] raised their blade.

The fissure was the source. Whatever came through it next would meet the same end as everything else.

The white light surged.

The golden dust spiraled upward, indifferent.

[[Legend]] planted their stance.

The wall opened.

Not swung. Not crumbled. Opened — and from the impossible angle it revealed, he stepped through mid-sentence. The amber light caught his coat, and the coat did not seem to notice. The dust drifted around him, and the dust did not seem to touch.

"—if geology is psychology, and geology is topology—"

[[Legend]] crossed the distance in two steps and swung.

He wasn't there.

He was behind them. Same extravagant coat. Same unfinished sentence. He hadn't dodged — the space between them had simply renegotiated itself, and he had continued talking through the entire process.

"—is psychology topology—"

[[Legend]] turned. Swung again.

Behind them again. His coat arrived a step after him.

"—but why psychology—"

He stopped.

He tilted his head at something to [[Legend]]'s left that wasn't there — his eyes tracking a slow lateral arc, the way someone watches a color move that has no business moving, a color that isn't in any spectrum the cathedral could account for. His lips moved once, soundlessly, as though filing a footnote no one had asked for.

A long pause — the kind with structure, the kind the silence around it has to brace for.

"Wait—"

He stared.

Not a pause. A _stare_ — the full, arrested, genuinely uncomprehending stare of a man who has just been told the sky is a direction. He looked at [[Legend]]. He looked at the wall. He looked back at [[Legend]]. He opened his mouth. He closed it. 

He turned fully toward the wall, studied it for a long moment with the expression of someone trying to be charitable about something that does not deserve charity, then turned back.

"Well no, you — you can't—" He stopped himself. He pressed his thumb to his lips. He tried again, with great care, as if explaining to a very sincere person that water is wet. "That wall is _clearly invisible._ That's not — I'm not going to argue again the ontology of visible surfaces with someone standing inside a cathedral that is also, I will note, not entirely a cathedral anymore, which raises its own — no." He shook his head once, sharply, as if dislodging something. 

"I have spent a _significant_ number of [[Cycle]]s debating the difference between a surface and the suggestion of it. I am not going over that again. Not here. Not in front of the clots."

He was already moving — not toward [[Legend]] but around them, in uneven circuits, coat lagging behind him at a slow, unbothered delay. The white clots drifted from his path with learned habit, as though they had long since learned better. His steps disturbed the golden dust in slow, lazy eddies. His eyes kept moving — sliding off [[Legend]], catching briefly on something at an angle that the room simply did not contain, the way someone tracks a bird only they can hear.

[[Legend]] turned with him. Blade still raised. He did not look at the blade.

"You came through the Anchor." Filed mid-stride, like a notation. "A new Cradle sends its cleaners. Very [[Civic]]."

He stopped. He turned.

"Are you pointing a blade at me right now."

"You came out of the same fissure as every [[Atonalis]] that just tried to kill me."

"Me." He said the word the way someone says the name of a distant relative they find personally embarrassing. "_An [[Atonalis]]._"

He looked down at himself. Conducted a brief inventory in the golden light — checking both hands first, front and back, as though verifying they were still the right kind of hands. Looked back up.

"I maintain a Library containing every thought ever had in the wrong order by the right person at the wrong time — which is, I will note, most of them." A pause with something private in it. "I once spent forty[[ Cycle]]s establishing whether a particular set of potatoes — a shepherd, something fourth Age, unremarkable region, terrible soil — but they were in fact the theological pivot point of an entire civilization. They were. The potatoes understood this about themselves. And—" He stopped. He looked briefly at something that was not the cathedral, not the forest, not [[Legend]]. "No wait, that's not how the story goes." A longer pause. He pressed two fingers to the bridge of his nose. "What am I talking about."

A beat.

"The point," he said, recovering his thread with the care of a man who had dropped it many times, "is that I have not given up, if not why would I be on a search? Just look at these poor things."

He gestured at the white clots, golden dust drifting lazily between them.

"Does that sound like an [[Atonalis]] to you?"

The light through the broken windows had begun to do something light in ruined cathedrals was not supposed to do.

It _gathered_ — slow golden spirals pulling inward, ambient dust tightening its orbit around him without urgency, the way iron filings find a magnet and choose to make it graceful. The white clots nearest him had stopped drifting.

They held, faintly luminous, cold against all that warm gold, like breath held inside a chest that had forgotten it was holding. Something in [[Legend]]'s own chest — low, resonant, below the frequency of thought — answered without asking first.

He pivoted. Pointed.

"Okay. There is something," he said, "that every [[Civilization]] in every single [[Moon]] has eventually found and stood before and been unable to name." He began pacing, slower now, the coat settling around him with a new gravity. "It is not a monument. It is not a temple. It does not distinguish itself in any way that should matter — and yet no [[Civilization]] that has ever found it has been able to destroy it, or leave it, or stop sending people back to stand at its edge and look."

A pause. His eyes caught on something at the periphery, that lateral slide again, tracking a color that had no business being in the conversation. He let it go. 

"When you look into it, it does not show you your face. It shows almost an [[Invisible Color]], or at least in a sense."

His voice had gone quieter, not softer. "Because nothing that goes in ever comes out. Not once. Every name every soul has ever borrowed from itself is still in there, suspended, waiting. Do you understand what that means? That it is _full_ — completely, permanently full — and it never _once_ had to be emptied to make room?"

He stopped walking. He turned. His expression had shifted into something unreadable — not absent. Too present.

"Do you understand what you are looking at when you look deep into the well? You are looking at the total weight of every self that has ever existed in this world and decided they needed a different face to survive. All of it. _Still there._ Still looking back. What do you think?"

He waited. A full, counted [[Beat]].

"I have no clue what that is."

He went very still.

Not confused. Not offended. The stillness of a man who has just found an empty chamber in an archive he has tended alone for longer than cities have had names. Above him, through the broken vault, the golden canopy swayed. The dust continued its slow spiral, patient, as though it had been waiting for exactly this.

He crossed the entire cathedral floor in the space between one heartbeat and the next — not running, not walking, _arriving_ — and both his hands came up and found either side of [[Legend]]'s face before the blade could rise, and his eyes were very close, and they were not a color, they were a phenomenon. Looking into them felt like staring through a telescope left open to the void — distance, and distance, and then impossibly, [[Legend]] themselves, from behind. The white clots rotated toward them both in a slow planetary drift, cold light against warm gold. The dust continued rising through the broken roof into open sky.

He said:

"Hold on to something."

#### The Transfer

The first sensation was warmth — not the amber warmth of the forest outside, but something older. Warmth arriving from every direction at once, the way a room feels when it has been lived in for a very long time by people who cared about each other.

Then pressure. Not pain — the pressure of too much arriving through a door sized for one thing at a time.

Then color. Not images. Color as _feeling_ — a blue so specific it carried something irrevocably left behind; a gold so thin it was the last moment before a flame goes out and knows it; a white that was not absence but everything still undecided, hovering just before form.

Then sound — not music, the _feeling_ of music. The resonance that lives in the chest after the last note ends and the hall goes silent and no one wants to be the first to move.

Then it — the thing he had described. Not seen. _Sensed._ And at its surface something that was not a reflection. Looking into it, [[Legend]] understood with sudden animal clarity that it was looking back, and that whatever it saw did not use the name [[Legend]] had always answered to.

And it was full.

Completely. Permanently. Full — and [[Legend]] felt the weight of that, felt the total accumulated mass of every self that had ever decided it needed a different face to survive, pressing up from somewhere with no bottom, patient, cold, and awake.

Then a question — arriving from every direction at once, in no language and all of them —

_— the question had no words here. Only the feeling of the asking._

[[Legend]] came back to the cathedral like a book being shelved.

On their knees. The flagstone was cold and unreasonably present. The golden dust still spiraled upward through the broken vault, slow and indifferent. "You'll have questions," he said. "The questions are correct."

He set the key on the flagstone between them. Not handed. _Placed_ — the way you place a coordinate, not a gift. Pale and translucent, edged in threadwork too fine to read at any distance, catching the amber light and doing nothing useful with it. It hummed at two tones below hearing, and something in [[Legend]]'s inner ear felt as though it had just remembered something it hadn't known it was carrying.

"For when you're ready. Not before."

### The [[Legend]] of Nowhere and the King of Madness

Due to his enigmatic nature and sometimes hazardous knowledge, [[The White-Haven Library]] is feared by some as a [[Primal Discordia]] [[Atonalis]], despite him being not one. This is due to three distinct primary reasons:

- **Memetic Hazards:** The knowledge he can offer can have devastating consequences, particularly when weaponized. [[The White-Touched Archivist]] himself sees knowledge as sterile but giving someone the tools to unleash a plague has devastating consequences, whether intentional or by accident a good amount of the truths he can give can become dangerous. Thus, some believe he's a curse incarnate and as dangerous, perhaps even more so, than regular [[Atonalis]]. Moreover, some of the people who have made it to [[The White-Haven Library]] become mad due to the sheer amount of information and the truths contained. _"Whomever comes in contact with this White Madman, becomes as Mad as the evil spirit itself."_

- **His Methods of Manifestation:** Both the link to enter the [[The White-Haven Library]] from [[Arcanoria]], and how [[The White-Touched Archivist]] appear is by clipping from seemingly impossible angles and objects are disturbing to look at. Moreover, clots of [[Primal White Noise]] leave temporary [[Vibrational Fallout]] on the exact point where he appears. This are dangerous and appear as a white version of [[Formless Masses]]. The appearance of these clots tends to be misinterpreted as anomalous behavior mirroring that of an [[Atonalis]], and whomever becomes mad by the knowledge tend to become an [[Atonalis]]. _"Hush now, when the silence appears laughter will die. Run now, far away from the madman before he takes you to the place in-between."_

- **The More Permanent Tethers are Filled with [[Atonalis]]:** This is a very paradoxical property because the strongest [[Resonance Anchors]] linking the impossible entrances of [[The White-Haven Library]] tend to be filled with [[Atonalis]] nests which makes a lot of regular people think he is the [[Atonalis]] King. This, however, could not be further from the truth. The [[Atonalis]] that appear nearby, particularly [[Anxithor]], do this because the demons themselves fear [[The White-Touched Archivist]]. The [[Atonalis]] are scared of him, and that's why they group in his [[Resonance Anchors]] because they want to wipe them out but since they are too dangerous to dismantle, they instead tend to group around in a permanent preemptive strike before [[The White-Touched Archivist]] appears or before anyone attempts to make contact with him. _"If there's a leader for all the demons, it has to be him and the [[Atonalis]] army that defends the doors of that forsaken palace."_
